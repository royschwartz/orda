Orda

Orda is a two-player turn-based strategy game — appearing as a board game, though one with anomalous aspects.

The pieces are shapeshifting cubes that tumble across the board when you move them, striking the board with echoing thuds. The pieces have faces on opposite sides that represent their unique identities. The faces are also how the pieces interact with the board — this usually happens through their mouths. They hum, spit, sing, and blow vapors.

In multiplayer, each game forms a STORY emerging from the interactions between game elements:

Examples

Swamps slow movement.

Forests reduce visibility.

Sky Season

In Sky, actions are more costly.

Null Season

In Null, Powers are stripped and Karma is granted.

Each Specimen has Powers which are dormant until revealed. The Hawk can fly 3 spaces.

The Siren can move a target closer.

The Scorpion may poison a hex.

The Mimic may copy Powers.

The Grey Elder is created with Karma and can pass through mountains and destroy enemies with a touch.

Wonders are game altering items created with Karma. The Director allows you to move nearby enemies.

The Injection lets a Specimen act twice per turn, but eventually destroys it.

The Bringer teleports Specimens.

The Entity is a controllable Poison cloud.

The Bomb destroys everything in the target hex, including the terrain itself.

Towers draw from energy sources and extend your power grid.

Sculptures regenerate the environment and make Karma during Null season.

Ancient sites with strange properties are littered across the map. The Gateway allows travel over great distances.

The Pool reveals the identities of concealed Specimens.

Single player entails a long match against an unseen opponent, with encounters occurring on different planets. The goal is to bring your Queen to a great blue light on the far end of the galaxy.

You can probably see from the presented game elements that they have an archetypal aspect. A frame story reveals the game to have been a creation of an alien civilization. The planetary conflicts and all the suffering they entail are very real, but in actuality merely echoes of a conflict playing out on a higher level of reality. The visible game is positioned between both of these worlds.
